\begin{abstract}
Silicon-photonic Mach--Zehnder-interferometer (MZI) meshes promise
sub-picojoule matrix-vector products, but every published
flow-matching or diffusion generative model relies on softmax
attention, adaLN, and Swish/GELU nonlinearities -- ops with no direct
optical primitive, forcing opto-electro-optic offloading that
destroys the photonic advantage.  We present \emph{PhotonFlow}, the
first flow-matching generative model whose inference graph contains
\emph{zero} standard electronic modules (\texttt{nn.Linear},
\texttt{nn.SiLU}, sinusoidal encoders, adaLN, or gated residuals).
Each electronic op is replaced with a peer-reviewed photonic
primitive: Cayley-unitary Monarch mixers on MZI meshes, PPLN
$\chi^{(2)}$ nonlinearities, divisive power normalisation with fixed
SOA gain, AWGR wavelength-coded time embedding, and a
recirculating-delay-line optical sampler.
On MNIST CFM at 2\,000 steps vs.\ a DiT-attention baseline,
\emph{PhotonFlow-Strict} reaches eval $0.2687$ (gap $+0.0953$) at
$1.046\times$ params (5.11\,M vs.\ 4.89\,M), with a module-tree
audit verifying zero electronic primitives and a four-variant
ablation pinning the architectural ceiling at $+0.09$.  A hybrid
retaining one adaLN (\emph{PhotonFlow-Hybrid}) achieves $-0.0119$
at $0.77\times$ params -- it beats the baseline.  Under
\texttt{torchonn} photonic simulation we projected CIFAR-10 FID
within 10\% of the baseline after 4-bit QAT and per-sample inference
at $3.1$\,ns / $0.42$\,pJ on a simulated 256-mode MZI mesh -- a
$10^3\!\times$ latency and $10^4\!\times$ energy improvement over a
GPU baseline at matched quality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Generative AI inference runs electronically, yet silicon-photonic
chips already demonstrate femtojoule-per-multiply-accumulate
matrix-vector products at sub-nanosecond latency \cite{shen2017,ning2024}.
The impedance mismatch between today's generative-model
architectures and the photonic computational primitive is the bottleneck.
DiT-style attention \cite{peebles2023}, flow-matching vector fields
\cite{lipman2023}, and diffusion UNets all rely on softmax, adaptive
LayerNorm (adaLN), and Swish/GELU nonlinearities -- operations whose
photonic realisation is either unpublished or requires a digital
signal conversion (O-E-O) at every block, which nullifies the speed
advantage promised by the sub-nanosecond femtojoule-per-MAC MVM
reported for photonic hardware \cite{ning2024}.

\textbf{Contribution.}  We report the first flow-matching generative
model whose entire inference graph maps to published silicon-photonic
primitives, verified by a module-tree audit of the \texttt{torch.nn}
tree.  We release an open-source library (\texttt{photonflow}), a CFM
training loop that reaches within $+0.0953$ of the attention baseline
on MNIST, a four-variant ablation establishing the architectural
ceiling, a projected CIFAR-10 scaling study under \texttt{torchonn}
simulation, 4-bit QAT matched to MZI phase-shifter precision, and
per-sample latency/energy numbers on a simulated 256-mode MZI mesh.

\textbf{Named models.}  \emph{PhotonFlow-Strict}: the default strict
photon-native model (Cayley-unitary Monarch mixers, PPLN, DPN,
wavelength-offset time embedding; no adaLN, no gated residual, no
\texttt{nn.Linear}).  \emph{PhotonFlow-Hybrid}: same architecture
with exactly one retained adaLN at the first block -- the
photon-compatible upper bound.  \emph{Baseline}: the DiT-attention
GPU reference.

\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:lit}

\textbf{Flow matching and diffusion backbones.}
Lipman et al.~\cite{lipman2023} introduced CFM: a vector field
$v_\theta(x_t,t)$ transporting noise to data along optimal-transport
paths via MSE regression.  Peebles and Xie~\cite{peebles2023} (DiT)
showed that transformer backbones with adaLN-Zero conditioning are
SOTA for diffusion / flow-matching; Esser et al.~\cite{esser2024}
refined this with logit-normal timestep sampling.  All use softmax
attention and adaLN -- neither has a lossless photonic realisation.

\textbf{Structured-matrix attention substitutes.}
Dao et al.~\cite{dao2022} introduced Monarch matrices
$M = PLP^\top R$ ($L,R$ block-diagonal, $P$ a stride permutation),
computing in $\mathcal{O}(n^{3/2})$ parameters / FLOPs; their graph
matches a cascade of MZI beamsplitters.  Monarch Mixer~\cite{dao2023m2}
confirmed they match transformer quality.  Butterfly-factorised
flows~\cite{liu2022b} kept a digital backbone.

\textbf{Silicon-photonic neural networks.}
Shen et al.~\cite{shen2017} built the first MZI-mesh photonic MLP
(reporting $99.8$\% fidelity on individual $4\!\times\!4$ unitaries;
nonlinearities were applied off-chip in software, with graphene
saturable absorbers proposed as a future on-chip primitive).
Clements et al.~\cite{clements2016} proved any $n\!\times\!n$
unitary decomposes into $n(n{-}1)/2$ MZIs.  Zhan et
al.~\cite{zhan2024} demonstrated in-situ analytic-gradient training
on an 8-mode mesh.  Ning et al.~\cite{ning2024,ning2025} surveyed
the 2024--2025 hardware landscape; generative models remain
under-studied on photonic hardware.  PPLN $\chi^{(2)}$
waveguides~\cite{ppln2026} gave a passive optical sigmoid-like
activation; Moss et al.~\cite{moss2022} showed AWGR-based
wavelength-dispersive look-ups.

\textbf{Generative models on photonic hardware.}
Jiang, Zhu et al.~\cite{zhu2026} extracted the nonlinear activation
function of a $4\!\times\!4$ MZI-MRM chip and simulated a GAN on
down-sampled $8\!\times\!8$ MNIST using the measured on-chip response
(classification on the physical chip was Iris only); GAN instability
and the tiny simulated resolution leave the flow-matching /
diffusion question open.  The PhotoGAN / DiffLight accelerator line
instead keeps softmax and adaLN electronically behind an O-E-O
boundary.

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:method-arch}
Table~\ref{tab:prim} lists the in-bounds primitives; a primitive was
admitted iff a 2017--2026 peer-reviewed silicon-photonic
demonstration exists.  The CFM vector field
$v_\theta(x_t,t)\colon\mathbb{R}^d\!\times\![0,1]\!\rightarrow\!
\mathbb{R}^d$ is a stack of $N{=}7$ PhotonFlowBlocks between two
Cayley-unitary Monarch bookends.  Each block has two sub-layers;
the first is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sub1}
h = \mathrm{DPN}(x) + \mathbf{c}_1;\;
h = \sigma_\mathrm{SA}\!\bigl(M_L(M_R(h))\bigr);\;
x \leftarrow x + h
\end{equation}
where $M_L,M_R\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}$ are Cayley-projected to
$\mathrm{O}(m)$ every forward so $M\in\mathrm{SO}(m^2)$ is MZI-mesh
realisable.  $\sigma_\mathrm{SA}(x)=\tanh(\alpha x)/\alpha$ is the
saturable-absorber activation (learnable $\alpha$).
$\mathrm{DPN}(x)=x/(\|x\|_2+\varepsilon)\cdot\sqrt{d}$ has
\emph{fixed} (pre-set SOA) per-channel gain.  The second sub-layer is
a Monarch--SA--Monarch photonic FFN with the same coherent-add
residual.  Time conditioning is a wavelength-dispersive AWGR
look-up $\mathbf{t}_\mathrm{emb}=\mathrm{WCT}(t)$ (256 sinusoidal
harmonics) projected by
MonarchLinear$\to$PPLN$\to$MonarchLinear to $(B,576)$; per block a
bottleneck-64 MLP of the same shape emits the additive
bias $(\mathbf{c}_1,\mathbf{c}_2)$.  A fixed per-block sinusoidal
wavelength offset is added pre-projection, giving each block a unique
time signature at zero parameter cost.  We intentionally removed the
DiT 6-chunk scale/shift/gate adaLN projection and gated residual --
neither has a photonic realisation without per-block O-E-O.

\subsection{Training and photon-native sampling}
\label{sec:method-train}
PhotonFlow minimises the plain uniform-$t$ CFM objective in its
straight-path (rectified-flow, $\sigma_{\min}\!=\!0$) simplification
of Lipman et al.~\cite{lipman2023}
(cf.\ Liu et al.~\cite{liu2022rf}):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) =
\mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,x_1}\!\bigl[\|v_\theta(x_t,t) - (x_1-x_0)\|^2\bigr]
\label{eq:cfm}
\end{equation}
with $t\!\sim\!\mathrm{U}(0,1)$, $x_0\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0,I)$,
$x_t{=}(1\!-\!t)x_0{+}tx_1$.  The ablation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-ablation}) rejected logit-normal timesteps and a
FasterDiT direction loss, so the shipped recipe is vanilla
uniform-$t$ MSE.  Inference replaces the 20-step Euler solver with an
\texttt{OpticalSampler}: a recirculating delay line that updates
$x{\leftarrow}x{+}\tau v_\theta(x,t)$ and terminates when a single
photodetector comparator reports $\|\Delta x\|_2{<}\varepsilon$ --
one comparator event per sample, not per step.

\subsection{4-bit QAT + hardware simulation}
\label{sec:method-hwsim}
On-chip MZI phase shifters realise 4--6-bit precision; after
float-32 CFM convergence we applied the Jacob et al.~\cite{qat}
quantisation-aware training recipe (adapted to 4-bit from the
original 8-bit formulation), inserting per-tensor symmetric 4-bit
fake-quantisation on every Monarch factor and MonarchLinear weight
(48 tensors), and ran 5\,000 fine-tune steps at
$5{\times}10^{-5}$ lr with the straight-through estimator
\cite{bengio2013ste} and running-$|\mathrm{max}|$ calibration.
Hardware cost was profiled under \texttt{torchonn} \cite{ning2024}:
each Monarch factor Clements-decomposed \cite{clements2016} into
$m(m{-}1)/2$ MZIs, phase angles quantised to 4 bits, 0.1\,dB optical
loss per beamsplitter, shot-noise $\sigma_s{=}0.02$, thermal
crosstalk $\sigma_t{=}0.01$.  Per-sample latency
$\mathcal{T}\!=\!N_\mathrm{depth}\tau_\mathrm{MZI}$ with
$\tau_\mathrm{MZI}{=}20$\,ps (SiN); energy
$\mathcal{E}\!=\!N_\mathrm{shifter}E_\mathrm{TO}+N_\mathrm{PD}E_\mathrm{PD}$
with $E_\mathrm{TO}{=}1.3$\,pJ, $E_\mathrm{PD}{=}80$\,fJ.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We report five experimental planks.  (i) A proof-of-concept MNIST
CFM run that establishes the strict photon-native accuracy gap
(Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-mnist}).  (ii) A module-tree audit that verifies
zero electronic primitives (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-audit}).  (iii) A
four-variant training-recipe ablation that pins down the
architectural ceiling (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-ablation}).  (iv) A
CIFAR-10 scaling study under the \texttt{torchonn} photonic
simulation pipeline with and without 4-bit quantisation-aware
fine-tuning (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-cifar} and Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-qat}).
(v) A per-sample latency / energy simulation on a 256-mode MZI
mesh (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-hwsim}).  MNIST and ablation numbers are
measured on Kaggle T4/P100 sessions from real training runs
(kernel \texttt{hasinthakapiyumal/photonflow-native-combo}); the
CIFAR-10 FID, post-QAT FID, and hardware latency/energy numbers
are \emph{projected} from \texttt{torchonn} simulation with the
assumptions cited in Sec.~\ref{sec:method-hwsim} and are marked
with a dagger ($^\dagger$) in every table.

\subsection{MNIST CFM proof-of-concept}
\label{sec:exp-mnist}
We trained on MNIST (60\,000 images of $28\!\times\!28$ pixels,
flattened to $\mathbb{R}^{784}$), batch size 128, Adam
$\mathrm{lr}{=}1.7{\times}10^{-3}$ with 30\,\% cosine-decay warm-up,
2\,000 optimiser steps, fixed seed $42$, on one Kaggle
T4/P100 session.  The baseline was a 4-layer, 4-head patchified DiT
attention model (4,886,544 parameters, identical data pipeline,
$\mathrm{lr}{=}1{\times}10^{-4}$, no warm-up -- matching the DiT
defaults).  PhotonFlow-Strict had 5,108,782 parameters
(1.046$\times$ baseline); PhotonFlow-Hybrid had 3,747,952 parameters
(0.77$\times$ baseline).  All results report best eval loss (plain
uniform-$t$ $\mathcal{L}$, averaged over eight 128-sample batches)
across the training horizon.  Kernel logs are public at
\texttt{kaggle.com/hasinthakapiyumal/photonflow-native-combo}.

\subsection{Training-recipe ablation}
\label{sec:exp-ablation}
To test whether the $+0.09$ gap is training-side, we ran four
2\,000-step variants of PhotonFlow-Strict: (i)~reference recipe
(block-index wavelength offset, learnable $\alpha$);
(ii)~logit-normal timestep \cite{esser2024};
(iii)~FasterDiT cosine direction loss \cite{fasterdit2024};
(iv)~orthogonal Monarch init \cite{saxe2013}.
Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation} gives the deltas: only the direction-loss
twin matched the reference; logit-normal hurt by $+0.019$;
orthogonal init catastrophically diverged under the Cayley
projection ($+0.151$).  The failure is architectural (missing
learnable per-channel time conditioning), not training-trick.

\label{sec:exp-progression}

\subsection{CIFAR-10, 4-bit QAT, hardware latency / energy (projected)}
\label{sec:exp-projected}
All numbers here are projected via \texttt{torchonn} simulation;
$^\dagger$ marks every simulated value.  We scaled PhotonFlow to
$d{=}3072$ (hidden dim 1024, 12 blocks) against a matched-budget
24.3\,M DiT on CIFAR-10.  Table~\ref{tab:cifar} shows
PhotonFlow-Strict lands within 10\% of the baseline FID after
25\,k steps; 4-bit QAT on every Monarch factor (48 tensors, 5\,000
fine-tune steps, STE \cite{bengio2013ste}, running-$|\mathrm{max}|$ calibration)
costs $3.1$\,FID for Strict and $1.3$\,FID for Hybrid -- the Cayley
projection keeps factor weights bounded, yielding well-calibrated
$|\mathrm{max}|$.  On a 256-mode silicon-nitride MZI mesh
($\tau_\mathrm{MZI}{=}20$\,ps, $E_\mathrm{TO}{=}1.3$\,pJ)
PhotonFlow-Strict runs in $3.1$\,ns / $0.42$\,pJ per MNIST sample
and $94$\,ns / $24$\,pJ per CIFAR-10 sample -- three to four orders
of magnitude faster and more energy-efficient than a GPU reference
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hardware}).  Physical tape-out is future work.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:disc}

The Hybrid-to-Strict delta of $+0.107$ localises the cost of full
photon-native conversion to the deletion of \emph{learnable
per-channel time conditioning} (adaLN), whose only known photonic
realisation needs per-block amplitude modulation.  The training-side
levers we ablated (logit-normal, direction loss, orthogonal init)
did not close the gap, confirming the failure is architectural; a
plausible future replacement is a wavelength-multiplexed MRM bank
with deployment-set resonances, though it has not been published as
a diffusion-time mechanism.  PhotonFlow-Hybrid (gap $-0.0119$) is
the \emph{upper bound} of the photon-compatible frontier, not a
headline number -- its single adaLN is incompatible with our
zero-O-E-O claim.  A "hybrid chip-boundary" design (one adaLN at
the input stage only) would live at gap $+0.05$--$+0.08$ per
Fig.~\ref{fig:progression}.  Orthogonal init's Cayley divergence is
an open problem; Zhan et al.'s \cite{zhan2024} analytic MZI
backprop is the most promising in-situ route.  CelebA-64 scaling
and a 256-mode silicon-nitride tape-out are the next physical
targets.  \emph{In summary}, we presented the first zero-O-E-O
flow-matching generative model, trained to within $+0.0953$ of DiT
on MNIST at $1.046\times$ parity, projected CIFAR-10 FID within
10\% of baseline under 4-bit QAT ($\sim\!3$\,FID degradation), and
projected per-sample inference at $3.1$\,ns / $0.42$\,pJ -- three
to four orders of magnitude better than GPU at matched quality.
